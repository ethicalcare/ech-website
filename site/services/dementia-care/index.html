Dementia Care

Could this help your family?

This care may be worth considering when…

  • A person experiencing dementia, memory changes or disorientation
  • A family trying to maintain familiar routines at home
  • A primary caregiver who needs consistent backup or respite
  • A family living far away that needs reliable local observations and updates

What support can look like

Care shaped around the person’s day.

  • Support with familiar routines and meaningful engagement
  • Personal care and daily-living assistance within the care plan
  • Consistent caregiver matching whenever possible
  • Individual routines for later-day changes, leaving-home concerns or other known risks
  • Family communication and review as needs change
Dementia Care

How the nurse-led approach supports this care

A nursing perspective when needs, risks or routines change.

A nurse can help look at the whole picture when memory changes, health needs, medications or safety concerns overlap. This can help the family understand which needs belong in everyday support and when another clinician should be involved.

Clear expectations

What your family should know before deciding.

  • Home may remain the right place for many people, but the level of support must match the person’s needs, risks and home environment.
  • Supervision, overnight care and concerns about leaving home need an individual conversation before care is arranged.
  • A sudden change in confusion, behaviour or health may need prompt clinical or emergency assessment.

A simple way to begin

Your family should know what happens next.

  1. Tell us what is happening

    Share what the person wants, what feels harder and when help may be needed.

  2. Talk through the options

    We will explain which kinds of support may be worth considering.

  3. Know who will do what

    Your family will know what a caregiver, nurse or other professional would provide.

  4. Stay in touch as needs change

    Let us know when something changes so the support can be looked at again.

Clear answers

Questions about dementia care

Can dementia care help someone remain at home?

Home support may help maintain routines and daily living, but suitability depends on the person’s needs, risks, environment, available family support and current care capacity.

Why does caregiver consistency matter?

Seeing a familiar caregiver can make routines feel less disruptive and can help small changes stand out more clearly. Ethical Care aims for continuity whenever possible, while being honest that scheduling and care needs can sometimes require another caregiver.

What if my loved one’s needs increase?

Please tell the care team early when routines, mobility, sleep, behaviour or health begin to change. We can help clarify who should review the concern and whether the care approach, schedule or clinical support needs another look.
