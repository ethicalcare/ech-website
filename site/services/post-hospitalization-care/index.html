A clearer transition home

Post-hospital care for the first days home and the recovery plan that follows.

Coming home from hospital can feel like a relief. It can still leave the family with a long list of questions. Post-hospital care can help prepare the home, support daily routines and connect nursing or rehabilitation when needed, so everyone understands the first days home and what happens next.

Post-Hospitalization Care

Could this help your family?

This care may be worth considering when…

  • Someone returning home after hospital care or surgery
  • A family arranging support on short notice
  • A person who needs help with mobility, meals, personal care or appointments
  • A family coordinating nursing, equipment or rehabilitation questions

What support can look like

Care shaped around the person’s day.

  • Discharge-day and post-surgical daily-living support
  • Meals, personal care, mobility, reminders, errands and appointments
  • Equipment, therapy and home-safety coordination
  • Qualified nursing care when it is clinically appropriate and currently available
  • A clear understanding of what Ethical Care, the family and any outside professional will handle

How the nurse-led approach supports this care

A clearer handoff from discharge instructions to care at home.

A nurse can help the family make sense of the discharge information they have been given, identify which needs require clinical follow-up and keep nursing responsibilities separate from everyday home support. Ethical Care follows the person’s hospital discharge and medical instructions; it does not replace the hospital or prescriber.

Clear expectations

What your family should know before deciding.

  • Some nursing treatments need a prescriber’s instructions, the right supplies and a qualified nurse.
  • When care can start depends on the discharge information, the support needed and current availability.
  • For urgent or worsening symptoms, contact the appropriate clinician or emergency service.

A simple way to begin

Your family should know what happens next.

  1. Tell us what is happening

    Share what the person wants, what feels harder and when help may be needed.

  2. Talk through the options

    We will explain which kinds of support may be worth considering.

  3. Know who will do what

    Your family will know what a caregiver, nurse or other professional would provide.

  4. Stay in touch as needs change

    Let us know when something changes so the support can be looked at again.

Clear answers

Questions about post-hospitalization care

When should we contact Ethical Care about a discharge?

As early as possible, ideally before the discharge date. Share the expected timing, location and the support the hospital says will be needed, and the team can explain the planning steps and confirm current availability.

Can this include nursing or wound care?

It may. A nurse first needs to understand the treatment instructions, supplies and current care needs, then confirm who can provide the care.

Can Ethical Care coordinate rehabilitation?

Ethical Care may be able to help connect the family with an appropriate rehabilitation professional. Before you decide, we will explain who provides the service, how information is shared with consent and who bills for the care.
