Companion Care & Home Support

Could this help your family?

This care may be worth considering when…

  • Someone spending long periods alone or becoming less connected
  • A family living at a distance that needs trusted local support
  • A person who needs help with routines, errands, meals or appointments
  • A family seeking consistent support before needs become more complex

What support can look like

Care shaped around the person’s day.

  • Conversation, meaningful activities and social connection
  • Light homemaking, laundry, linens, meals and hydration support
  • Shopping, errands and accompaniment to appointments when included in the care arrangement
  • Safety check-ins, routine observations and family updates
  • Consistent caregiver matching whenever possible

How the nurse-led approach supports this care

A nursing perspective when needs are changing or unclear.

Companion care is usually provided by a caregiver, not a nurse. A nursing perspective can still be helpful when everyday observations suggest that your loved one’s health or level of support may need another look.

Clear expectations

What your family should know before deciding.

  • If outings or appointments are part of the plan, ask how transportation, mobility help and expenses will be handled.
  • Money, shopping and prescription pickup should always follow clear safeguards agreed with the family.
  • Companion care does not replace nursing care, medical advice or emergency help.

A simple way to begin

Your family should know what happens next.

  1. Tell us what is happening

    Share what the person wants, what feels harder and when help may be needed.

  2. Talk through the options

    We will explain which kinds of support may be worth considering.

  3. Know who will do what

    Your family will know what a caregiver, nurse or other professional would provide.

  4. Stay in touch as needs change

    Let us know when something changes so the support can be looked at again.

Clear answers

Questions about companion care

Can companion care include help outside the home?

It may include errands, appointments or community activities when those plans are agreed in advance. We will talk through transportation, mobility needs and how any purchases should be handled.

Will the same caregiver visit?

Familiarity matters, especially when a person values a steady routine. Ethical Care aims for caregiver continuity whenever possible, although schedules, availability and changing needs can affect who visits.

How can our family stay informed if we live far away?

With the person’s consent, the care arrangement can identify one family contact and the kinds of updates that would be helpful. We will explain how routine updates are shared and who to contact when a question comes up.
