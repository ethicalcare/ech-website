Reliable relief for primary caregivers

Respite care that protects the caregiver without disrupting the person’s routine.

Respite care gives a primary caregiver or support person time to rest, attend appointments, look after their own health or step away for a while. The person receiving care keeps a familiar routine, and the family knows who will be there and what support will be provided.

Family Respite Care

Could this help your family?

This care may be worth considering when…

  • A primary caregiver whose own health or energy is changing
  • A spouse, adult child or support person who cannot provide the same level of care
  • A family needing scheduled breaks, appointment coverage or travel support
  • A caregiver seeking backup before reaching a crisis point

What support can look like

Care shaped around the person’s day.

  • Short scheduled breaks or recurring respite visits
  • Temporary coverage during appointments, travel or family obligations
  • Companion, personal or dementia support based on the care plan
  • Overnight respite when the person’s needs, staffing and overnight arrangement can be confirmed
  • Clear handoff information before and after the respite period

Family Respite Care

How the nurse-led approach supports this care

Clear roles help the person, family and care team work together.

A nurse may be helpful when the person receiving care has health needs that affect the respite arrangement. The family should know which support a caregiver will provide and whether any part of the visit requires a nurse.

Clear expectations

What your family should know before deciding.

  • Respite is planned coverage, not emergency backup.
  • Overnight respite depends on staffing, rest rules and the person’s needs.
  • Clinical procedures require an appropriately qualified professional.

A simple way to begin

Your family should know what happens next.

  1. Tell us what is happening

    Share what the person wants, what feels harder and when help may be needed.

  2. Talk through the options

    We will explain which kinds of support may be worth considering.

  3. Know who will do what

    Your family will know what a caregiver, nurse or other professional would provide.

  4. Stay in touch as needs change

    Let us know when something changes so the support can be looked at again.

Clear answers

Questions about respite care

Do I have to wait until I am overwhelmed?

No. Planned respite can be considered earlier as part of a sustainable care arrangement.

Can respite include personal or dementia care?

It may. The person’s needs should be understood in advance and matched with a caregiver who has the right training and experience.

Can respite be overnight?

It may be possible. We first need to understand what help is needed overnight, whether the caregiver must remain awake and which staffing arrangement is currently available.
