Clear answers

Home-care questions from families

01What is private home care?

Private home care is arranged and paid for directly by the person or family rather than provided as part of a publicly subsidized program. It may add help with daily routines, companionship, family respite, nursing, rehabilitation or scheduling beyond the support already in place.

02How is private care different from publicly subsidized home support?

Public home support is arranged through the health system based on assessment, eligibility and available programs. Private care is arranged directly with a provider. Some families use one or the other, while others combine them to fill gaps in timing, tasks or continuity.

03What does nurse-led home care mean at Ethical Care?

A registered nurse can help make sense of health and day-to-day needs, guide assessment or planning, provide specific nursing care and help the family understand who is responsible for each part. It does not mean a nurse provides every visit.

04How much does home care cost?

Cost depends on the type of support, the qualifications required, the number and timing of visits, the location and how soon care may need to begin. Ask for a written explanation of what is included, any minimum hours and what could change the cost.

05Why does Ethical Care create a care plan?

A care plan turns a general request for help into something the person and family can understand. It should explain the person’s goals, what support will be provided, who will provide it, when visits happen and what to do if needs change.

06Can Ethical Care help with medication or wound care?

Medication support and wound care can mean different things. Some help may be provided by a caregiver, while clinical assessment, medication administration or wound treatment requires an appropriately qualified nurse and the necessary instructions and supplies. Contact Ethical Care to discuss the specific need and current availability.

07Does Ethical Care offer overnight or 24-hour care?

Ethical Care can discuss awake overnight care, sleeping overnight arrangements and continuous care provided through rotating caregivers. The right option depends on what happens during the night, whether someone must remain awake, the home setup, staffing and current availability.

08Which communities does Ethical Care serve?

Ethical Care is based in Coquitlam and speaks with families across the Lower Mainland. Availability depends on the specific location, schedule, care needs and appropriate staff, so contact the team to confirm the address and timing.

09What happens after a family contacts Ethical Care?

The first conversation focuses on what the person wants, what has changed, which support is already in place, where care is needed and how soon help may be required. The team can then explain the most appropriate next step and confirm current availability.

10How are caregivers chosen for a family?

A good match considers the person’s needs, routines, preferences, communication style, location and schedule, along with the caregiver’s training and experience. Ask Ethical Care how screening, references, training and ongoing support are handled for the person who may provide care.

11What happens if my loved one’s needs change?

Tell the care team when a new symptom, difficult routine, safety concern or change in what the family can manage appears. The appropriate person can review what has changed and explain whether the plan, schedule, nursing involvement or another service needs to be considered.

12How can our family stay informed?

With the person’s consent, the care arrangement can identify a main family contact, the kinds of updates that would be helpful and how urgent concerns should be shared. Ask who to contact with questions and how routine updates will be provided.
